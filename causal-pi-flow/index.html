Abstract

Distilling autoregressive (AR) video diffusion models into few-step generators typically proceeds in two stages: teacher forcing on clean histories, followed by self-forcing on student rollouts. While FlowMap distillation methods—such as consistency models and π-Flow—can in principle train both stages directly from the AR teacher, existing approaches still fall short of Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) in visual quality and rely on DMD for self-forcing at the expense of sample diversity. We trace this gap to a failure mode of teacher guidance in each stage. First, during teacher forcing, conditioning on the clean history resolves the static background, concentrating uncertainty in moving regions where standard Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) fails to reduce the curvature of the teacher's velocity field. Second, during self-forcing, the causal teacher is trained only on clean histories; when conditioned on a drifted student rollout, it continues the trajectory rather than pulling it back toward the clean manifold. We address both failure modes by modifying only the teacher guidance. For teacher forcing, we introduce motion-aware skip-layer guidance, which predicts patch-wise flow-matching uncertainty from intermediate teacher features and selectively guides high-uncertainty moving regions. For self-forcing, we introduce anti-drifting guidance, which pairs the frozen teacher with an online drift model updated on student rollouts to steer trajectories back toward the clean manifold. Across chunk-wise and frame-wise causal teachers on Wan2.1-1.3B, applying our two guidance methods to π-Flow and dCM raises VBench by up to +3.15 points over CFG, matching DMD-based baselines in 4 NFE while substantially improving multi-seed diversity and 30-second rollout stability.

Why does FlowMap distillation fall short on autoregressive video models?

An autoregressive (AR) video model generates a video chunk by chunk, and each chunk reads the previous ones through the key–value (KV) cache. Analysis: why doesn't CFG fix Stage 1?

We measure how smoothly the guided teacher's velocity varies between nearby states with the normalized sensitivity Cv; a 4-step student can follow a smooth (low-Cv) field. In the first chunk, which has no history, CFG raises the smoothness 1/Cv by up to 7.7%. In the second chunk it raises it by at most 0.3% and lowers it as guidance grows.

The reason is that the history already fixes the static background and largely satisfies the text prompt, so the remaining uncertainty sits in moving regions. A single teacher step from noise already gives a sharp background and a blurred moving person. CFG only strengthens the text condition and cannot steer frame-to-frame motion; uniform skip-layer guidance perturbs static and moving regions alike and saturates at +2.5%.

Method: change only the teacher's target

Both failures come from the target v*, so we change only v*. The FlowMap loss and the student stay the same, and the distilled student still samples in 4 steps with no guidance at inference.

Stage 1: Motion-aware skip-layer guidance

Analysis: where the uncertainty goes, and what it buys

The uncertainty head is a single Transformer layer on detached features of block ℓ = 10 of the frozen teacher, trained for 500 updates with a Gaussian negative log-likelihood on the teacher's flow-matching residual. Its prediction ûp is high everywhere in the first frame, which has no history, and concentrates on the moving people afterwards.

The prediction is smoothed with a 3×3 mean pool and normalized per chunk index and noise level into a skip strength up = max(0, ū + σu(ûp − μ)/σ), with ū = σu = 0.5. In the second chunk, motion-aware guidance raises whole-chunk smoothness 1/Cv by +3.4% (CFG: at most 0.3%; uniform skip-layer guidance: +2.5%). The gain grows with the predicted uncertainty and reaches +16.4% over CFG on the most uncertain tenth of the patches.

Stage 2: Anti-drifting guidance

How is this different from DMD?

Both contrast the teacher with a model fit to the student's rollouts, but they act at different places. DMD minimizes the mode-seeking reverse KL at the clean output: it re-noises the student's final video with fresh noise and backpropagates the score difference into it, which discards the coupling between a noise sample and its video. Anti-drifting guidance keeps the deterministic coupling of FlowMap distillation and changes only the teacher's velocity at the intermediate noisy state of each chunk, along the denoising trajectory. Adding (γ − 1)(vϕ − vψ) shifts that velocity in proportion to the log density ratio between the teacher's and the student's distributions, which steers the trajectory back toward the data. We use γ = 5, only in Stage 2 and after the first chunk.

30-Second Long-Video Generation

We generate 30-second videos (501 frames) for the 128 MovieGen prompts of Deep Forcing, using its KV-cache management. Distilled from the Causal-rCM teacher without DMD, our students lose less imaging quality over 30 s than Causal-rCM (ΔIQ −1.64 for π-Flow and −1.97 for dCM vs. −3.28), and our π-Flow student has the highest aesthetic quality of all students (62.13 AQ). Causal Forcing keeps its imaging quality slightly better (ΔIQ −1.44) but has lower aesthetic quality (58.71) and a larger aesthetic drop (ΔAQ −5.59 vs. −3.83 for ours).

Preserving Multi-Seed Diversity Without DMD

Why distill directly from the causal teacher with FlowMap objectives instead of using Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD)? Because DMD minimizes a mode-seeking reverse-KL divergence, it severely collapses sample diversity across random seeds—producing nearly identical layouts, camera angles, and object trajectories for a given prompt.

By eliminating DMD entirely and supervising self-forcing via trajectory-consistent FlowMap distillation with anti-drifting guidance, our π-Flow students are more diverse than every corresponding SF-DMD student in V-JEPA 2 distance (chunk-wise: +74% over Self Forcing and +29% over Causal Forcing; frame-wise: +56% over Causal Forcing). In the chunk-wise setting they even exceed the 64-NFE AR teacher (0.0964 vs. 0.0882), though not in the frame-wise setting (0.0800 vs. 0.0944):
